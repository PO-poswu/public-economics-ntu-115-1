Orient Your Audience From the Start

  1. What is my level of expertise relative to my audience?
  2. How do I want to change my readers’ view of my topic?
  3. How much does my audience already know about the problem/issue I am addressing? How much do they care about it?
  4. What’s the “news” in my paper? What constitutes old information and new information for my audience?
  5. How resistant is my audience to my thesis?
  6. How busy is my audience?

The adage ‘free as air’ has become obsolete by Act of Parliament. Neither air nor light have been free since the imposition of the window-tax. We are obliged to pay for what nature lavishly supplies to all, at so much per window per year; and the poor who cannot afford the expense are stinted in two of the most urgent necessities of life.

Dickens, Charles. 1850. Household Words, vol. 1. London: Bradbury and Evans.

Oates and Schwab (2015, JoEP)

  • Residents throughout England and Scotland boarded up windows to avoid the tax. In many houses, bricks took the place of previously existing windows.
  • Newly constructed dwellings economized on the number of windows. In at least one apartment building in Edinburgh, the entire second floor (containing bedrooms) had no windows at all.
  • Lack of proper ventilation and fresh air \(\Rightarrow\) propagation of dysentery, gangrene, and typhus.
